Native reference calibration

The same-matrix native Ridge reference was timed twice at each of 256 and 2,048 evaluations, with a fresh sklearn fit and native metrics per evaluation. The fitted cost model is

TLimen prepared(N) = 0.120492 + 0.006253191N seconds

The estimated intercept is for the prepared loop, which excludes Python startup and imports. The Rust denominator is the complete process. The full native CLI calibration measures its complete process on a separate 13-feature workflow.

For one billion sampled rows, the measured assembly process took 98.286879 s and the same-matrix native Ridge projection was about 72.37 days, giving a projected reference-cost ratio of about 63,622. The native reference retains its own signal and solver-failure conventions.

Variation in the calibration

The line assumes a constant per-evaluation cost. The two prepared-native repetitions imply slopes of about 5.897 and 6.609 ms per row, and do not measure long-run memory, artifact I/O or scheduling effects.

Score-cache saturation

Each of the 1,677 successful training keys has 2,006 rule and threshold combinations (4 × 401 + 2 × 201), for 3,364,062 possible valid base-score keys. The 100m sample reaches that ceiling. At one billion rows, about 99.54% of valid rows reuse a base score.

TRust(N) ≈ fixed training + c · unique scores(N) + dN

After unique scores stop increasing, the per-row work continues to grow. With a linear native reference cost, the structural model therefore tends to a finite time ratio.
